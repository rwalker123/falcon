Five ways to hold a knowledge screen that keeps growing

The panel is columns-per-domain with a pinned detail pane, and it no longer fits the screen. The cause is structural, not cosmetic: domains are the axis that grows, and they sit on the axis that cannot scroll. These five prototypes are judged on one question — what happens at four times today's tree. Use the stress bar to grow it.

The measurementWhy it overflows, in numbers

A domain column has a COLUMN_MIN_WIDTH floor of 210px and a COLUMN_SEPARATION of 20, and the detail pane is pinned at DETAIL_WIDTH 300 plus padding. So the content minimum is a straight function of the domain count:

card ≈ 230 × domains + 336 + chrome

The ladder config today teaches five branches — plant, animal, route, forestry, extraction — plus the craft fan, so six columns ask for about 1,716px before chrome. docs/plan_civilization_steps.md commits four more branches (Storage, Belief, Defense, Dwellings) and calls a sixth branch "config"; War and Telling are already named in DOMAIN_BRANCH_LABELS' neighbourhood as absent-for-now. Ten to twelve domains is the planned shape, not the stress case — and twelve columns is 3,096px, on a 1920 design viewport that docked panels have already taken a bite out of.

And the clamp that should have caught it cannot. KnowledgePanel.refit does raise max_width to the room before calling fit_width, and fit_width does clamp the request to it — but AutoSizingPanel._apply_width then writes that number to size.x, and Godot will not render a Control below its get_combined_minimum_size(). The card's combined minimum runs straight through KnowledgeScroll, whose horizontal_scroll_mode is SCROLL_MODE_DISABLED — a ScrollContainer that cannot scroll an axis propagates its child's full minimum on that axis rather than absorbing it. So the clamp is computed correctly and then overruled by the layout. No clamp will make the shipped arrangement fit; the content minimum itself has to come down, which is what every option below does.

That is the panel as it ships, with no cap and no scroll — the strip below the card is the screen edge it has already gone past. Grow the tree in the stress bar and watch the number.

The asymmetry is the whole design problem. The ladder is deliberately shallow — the rung engine models about four steps per web and the plan forbids pan, zoom and edge routing — and it grows by adding branches. So the bounded axis (depth) is on the scrollable one, and the unbounded axis (branches) is on the one that cannot move. Every option below is a way of swapping them.

Option A · your option 1Columns, with the strip scrolling sideways

Smallest possible change: turn the column strip's horizontal scroll back on, clamp the card to the room, keep the detail pane pinned. Nothing about the shipped renderer changes.

Cheapest to build Breaks the "filters dim, never hide" rule Dead by 10 domains

Grow the tree and the failure shows itself: a knowledge that is learning, unspent or new can sit entirely off-screen, and the player has no way to know it is there but to drag. The panel's own contract — non-matching nodes dim rather than disappear, so the shape of the tree stays legible — is silently void the moment content leaves the viewport. The "3 more →" cue patches the symptom and concedes the point.

Option B · your option 2Domains wrap into a grid of cards

Each domain becomes a card; the card count wraps at a computed columns-per-row, and growth goes into the vertical scroll the panel already has. Rails, filters, node rows and the detail pane are untouched — this is the least invasive option that actually scales.

Reuses every existing part Width is fixed forever At 16+ it is a wall, not a glance

Honest and cheap, and it survives the planned ten. Past that the weakness is legibility rather than geometry: four rows of cards is a scrolling field of fifty-odd nodes, and "what is happening right now" stops being answerable by looking. Note also that the cards have ragged heights — the craft fan is much taller than a two-rung ladder — so a computed column count beats a free flow, or the rows jitter as the tree changes.

Watch the node names as you grow it. Three cards across a card that also carries the detail pane leaves each one about 186px — under the 210px COLUMN_MIN_WIDTH floor — and Seed Selection starts ellipsizing. The floor exists for a stated reason (a node name that wrapped would break the column's read as a ladder), so B only works if the detail pane moves out of the row, or the card is allowed past 1,100px, or the grid drops to two columns and gets taller. That choice is B's real design question, and it is the same trade C avoids by not putting names in narrow boxes.

Option C · recommendedRotate it: one domain per row, rungs running across

The swap stated directly. A domain is a row: its label on the left, its rungs left to right along the rail, a capability that gates nothing hanging off the end. Width is bounded by ladder depth — which the design caps at about four — and every new branch costs one row of height on an axis that already scrolls.

Width bounded by design, not by luck 24 domains still read at a glance The rail still says "these are steps" The craft fan needs its own treatment

Grow this one to 24 and the difference from B is stark: one line per domain means the whole tree is still scannable, because scanning a column of labels is what eyes are good at. The detail opens inline beneath the selected row rather than in a pinned pane, which returns another 336px and lets the card sit at about 800px wide at any domain count.

Two things need deciding if you take this one. The craft fan is not a ladder — it is unordered and will grow with recipes.json, so it wraps as connector-less chips and may want its own section below the ladders rather than a peer row. And a capability like Foddering, which the config says no rung waits on, needs to read as hanging off the ladder rather than continuing it; shown here as a dashed capsule.

Option DA domain rail, one ladder at a time

Master–detail. A scrolling rail of domains on the left, each carrying its own state pips; the selected domain's ladder in the middle; detail on the right. The card is a fixed ~780px at six domains or at sixty.

Scales to any count, unconditionally Narrowest card of the five The whole-tree reading is gone

The one option that provably never overflows. The cost is the panel's stated purpose: the screen is "what your people know", and a rail answers "what do my people know about herds" instead. The pips carry some of the loss back — a domain with something learning or unspent says so without being opened — but a player must now click six times to take the reading they used to take by looking. Worth prototyping as the fallback if the domain count ends up past what a screen of rows can hold.

Option E · the scales-to-200 answerDrop the tree; sort by what is happening

No domain layout at all. One list, grouped by state — learning now, ready and unused, new this turn, known, not begun — with the domain as a chip on each row and ladder position as step 2 of 4. The filters stop being a dimming pass and become the navigation.

Indifferent to domain count Answers "what is happening" first Loses the ladder shape the rail exists to state

This is what the panel would be if it were designed for sixty nodes from the start, and it is included to mark the far end of the range. It directly contradicts a shipped decision — the rule file is explicit that a ladder domain's rail is what says these are steps in an order — so it is only the right answer if the tree grows past the point where shape is readable anyway. A plausible hybrid is C as the map with this as a second tab.

What I would take forward

C, with B as the fallback. C is the only option that both survives the planned tree and keeps the reading the screen exists to give, and it does so because it puts growth on the axis the design already made cheap. B is the safe version of the same insight — it also fixes the geometry permanently and costs almost nothing to build — and it is the right answer if the row treatment of the craft fan proves awkward.

A should not ship on its own at any tree size. Its one genuine contribution is that re-enabling KnowledgeScroll's horizontal scroll is what makes the card shrinkable at all — without it the clamp in refit is unenforceable. B, C, D and E do not need it, because each one brings the content minimum itself under the room; that is the difference between hiding the overflow and not having any.

D and E are the insurance. If the count really heads for sixty, D keeps the ladder shape and gives up the overview, E keeps the overview and gives up the shape. Neither needs building yet.
