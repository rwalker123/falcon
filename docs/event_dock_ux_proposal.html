Shadow-Scale · UX proposal · issue #272

Nothing in this game tells you a child was born.

The sim computes births, deaths and coming-of-age every single turn, and then throws all three away before anything can say so. This proposes the surface that would say so — a thin notification bar on the top or bottom edge, showing the few events that earned the space, opening to the whole log.

Replaces the hidden command feed (R) + the Inspector command stream Prototype interactive below
01

What exists, and why none of it is a notification system

The pipe is already built. What is missing is the data at one end and the surface at the other.

✓

The transport works and needs no schema change

CommandEventLog → CampaignSection.commandEvents → CommandFeedController.ingest_events, carrying {tick, kind, faction, label, detail}. kind is a schema string deliberately, so every new event kind below is a Rust enum variant and nothing more.

01

The demographic flows are computed, then discarded

systems/population.rs resolves births, maturation, aging and three separate death terms — and every one of them is a local binding that dies with the function. Only the post-turn bracket totals reach the cohort. There is no last_births, no last_deaths, nothing on the wire.

02

The one existing "population" signal is a diff, and diffs lie

The turn orb's losing-population alert compares two consecutive snapshots. It cannot tell a death from an emigration, cannot name a cause, and reports nothing at all when four births and four deaths cancel out in the same turn.

03

Two surfaces already read the feed, and neither is for the player

The HUD's left-dock command feed ships hidden behind R, six entries, capped at 40% of a dock column it shares with the selection card. The Inspector's Commands tab prefixes the same events with [SIM] inside a debug console that ships hidden too. Both are logs you go and find. A notification is the opposite: it finds you.

04

Every event is equally loud

Exactly two kinds — predator_raid and hunt_danger — render as alerts. A wolf raid, a barley sowing and an order acknowledgement otherwise arrive in the same weight, in the same place, at the same size.

02

Three questions, and the bar answers all three

Every notification system has to decide what a thing is, how loudly it says it, and how much the player wants to hear. Keeping those three separate is what stops the design collapsing into one undifferentiated stream.

What it is — the channel

World events come from the sim: a birth, a raid, a discovery, an expedition home. System events come from the client's own plumbing: the command socket dropped, a command was rejected, the server rolled back. Those live in an Inspector console the player never opens — but a lost connection is exactly the kind of thing a player must be told about, so it becomes a channel here rather than being deleted.

The client mints two kinds of its own, and the split is what makes the System channel worth reading. system is a fault or a state change: a refused command, a lost or restored socket, a resync forced by a frame that could not be applied, and the HUD's own refusals (Quick-hunt · No idle workers to assign). command_echo is an acknowledgement — Advance 1 turn., Answered the question., Stop improving (12, 8). — which restates an action the player took through the UI a second ago. The boundary is exact: a command accepted for sending is an echo; everything else is a fault.

The bar ignores command_echo entirely, through HudEventVocab.IGNORED_KINDS, and it does so at ingest — such an event is never stored, so it cannot appear at any detail level (Everything included), on either channel, in the bar or in the expanded log. It is not a rung and not a floor: a receipt for a button pressed a moment ago is not news, and it was crowding the very first turns of a new game. Ignoring is a display filter on this one surface — the event still exists, the Inspector's debug console still prints every one of them, and a mod may still read them.

How loudly — the importance rung

Three rungs, and a kind sits on exactly one. The rung decides the accent, the glyph, and which events win a slot when the bar is showing fewer than it has.

Alert

Something was lost, or needs you.

Crimson or amber, glyph-led, and it holds its slot until you have seen it.

predator_raid · hunt_danger
status=feral · status=lapsed
system: socket lost · command rejected

Notable

The world changed in a way worth knowing.

Signal cyan. The default floor — this is what most players will see most of the time.

born · died · migrated · came_of_age
site_discovered · found_settlement
campaign_milestone · campaign_victory
expedition_arrived · expedition_returned · tame

Routine

A receipt for a thing you asked for.

Dim ink, no accent rail. Off by default — this is the current feed's entire contents.

forage · hunt · sow
cultivate · corral · scout
cancel_order · expedition_sent

How much — the detail level

The player's preference is a floor on the rung ladder, not a separate taxonomy — which is what keeps it to three legible options instead of a checklist of twenty-seven kinds. Channel toggles sit alongside it in the expanded view, so a player who wants world events but not socket chatter can say so without touching the floor.

What building it changed

At four rows with the log open, the bar printed the log's own first turn-group a second time, directly beneath it — the same four events twice, six inches apart. So the bar is now strictly the collapsed state: opening the log replaces the rows with a one-line title, and the log's newest turn-group is the recent list. Set Recent to 4 and open it to see the version that survived.

The other thing only a running prototype shows: at "Everything" the reserved strip tries to eat the map. The real client already has the answer — the same cap DockScrollFit applies to the command feed today — so the strip yields and the log scrolls internally rather than the viewport shrinking toward nothing.

04

Retention is measured in turns, not entries

CommandEventLog holds 32 entries and drops the oldest. Add a birth, a death and a coming-of-age per band per turn and the ring evicts a wolf raid inside two turns — the cap would quietly eat exactly the events it exists to preserve.

So the bound becomes a turn window: the log keeps every event from the last retention_turns turns and drops whole turns off the back. A turn is the unit the player thinks in, the log groups by it, and the "Earlier turns" button walks backwards through it a few turns at a time.

LeverWhereDefaultWhy that number
retention_turns sim config 20 Long enough to answer "what happened while I was away", short enough to bound the wire.
recent_count client pref 2 Two rows is ~44px of reserved edge. Three is defensible; four starts to read as a panel.
detail_level client pref notable Alerts alone are too quiet to feel like a living world; everything is the current feed.
log_window_turns client pref 5 How many turns the expanded log shows before "Earlier turns" extends it.
→

So the log stops shipping whole, in this same change

The delta path ships command_events via diff_whole — the entire log re-serialises whenever any part of it changes, which is every turn. At 32 entries that was free. At 20 turns × ~10 events it is ~200 entries every turn, and the number grows with exactly the feature being added.

An append-only delta is therefore in scope here, not deferred. Each entry carries a monotonic seq; a delta ships only the entries appended since the baseline, plus the current base sequence so the client can drop what the sim has evicted. Widening the retention window and leaving it shipping whole would be the worst of both — a longer log costing more every turn to say the same thing.

05

What the sim has to start recording

Four new event kinds, three new accumulators, and one rule that makes the whole thing honest.

KindRungFires whenDetail tokens wire
born new Notable The birth accumulator crosses a whole person. Notable, not Routine — it shipped Routine, i.e. below the default floor, so a birth never appeared at all unless the player chose “Everything”, where it arrived buried among forage receipts. A population counter ticking up while the bar says nothing is the failure this whole proposal exists to remove. The rung is not a measure of how much a turn’s labour changed; it is whether the world changed in a way worth knowing, and the settlement gaining a person is the plainest such change there is. band= count=
died new Notable A bracket's death accumulator crosses a whole person. Notable, not Alert — bands routinely lose elders to cold, and a rung that interrupts for every one of them teaches the player to stop reading the bar. band= count= bracket= cause=
migrated new Notable lastEmigrated / lastImmigrated is non-zero. Whole counts already, so no accumulator. band= count= direction=
came_of_age new Notable The maturation accumulator crosses a whole person — a new pair of hands. band= count=

expedition_arrived Notable Already emitted. The other half of scout success. status= expedition=

The tokens are a contract, not a caption

The Detail tokens column above is what the sim sends: space-delimited key=value fragments, sized for a parser. They exist so the client can do work with them — join band= to whatever it calls that band, promote a status=feral row to the Alert rung — and they are never a string to display. The first build printed them verbatim and a row read category=settle_site at (64,36).

So the client renders them (EventDockPanel.detail_phrase): keys the label already carries are dropped (band, count, expedition), enumerated values get English (settle_site → Settle site, out → departed), coordinates keep their numbers and gain a space, and a numeric value keeps its key because Warriors 3 needs one where Category Settle site does not. Numbers are trimmed, never rounded: the sim writes casualties with {:.3}, so 2.000 renders as 2 while a genuinely fractional 1.750 stays 1.75 — rounding would state a precision the sim did not, and a casualty count reading 2 when the sim said 1.5 is a lie the player cannot detect. Anything with no table entry still comes out as English — underscores to spaces, first letter capitalised — which is the load-bearing part: new kinds and tokens arrive server-side with no schema change, so an unrecognised token is the common case over time and a raw identifier on screen has to be impossible by construction rather than by anyone remembering to add a row.

Births are a rate, not an event — so an accumulator turns one into the other

births = working0 × fertility is a Scalar. A band of thirty gets a fraction of a birth per turn, never one. Rounding per turn would either announce a birth in a band too small to have had one, or announce nothing for the whole game.

So each cohort carries three fractional accumulators — births, maturations, and deaths per bracket. Each turn adds that turn's flow; when an accumulator's integer part increments, one event fires naming count whole people and the accumulator keeps the remainder. Small bands get a birth every eight turns or so; large ones get several a turn. The deaths accumulator carries the cause that dominated the turn it crossed on, so cause=cold is the cold that actually killed rather than a re-derivation after the fact.

This is the same discipline the fertility factors already follow — the sim stores the inputs that drove a turn's outcome rather than letting the client re-derive them from a post-turn state that never produced them.

06

Decisions, settled

All six were reviewed and taken as recommended, with one amendment: a death is Notable, not an Alert. Each recommendation below is now the spec.

Does an alert hold its slot, or does the newest event always win?

Toggle Order in the prototype. Newest first keeps the bar chronological and readable, but a wolf raid can be pushed off by two forage receipts within a turn. Most important never loses the raid but makes the bar jump around, and the top row stops meaning "the latest thing".

Settled: newest-first, with one exception: an unread Alert pins to the leading slot and stays until the log is opened. You get chronology and you never lose the raid. That is the default the prototype boots in.

On a shared edge, which one hugs the screen edge?

The reserved-edge registry already stacks co-edge panels by priority. The bar can sit outside the band panel (hard against the screen edge) or inside it.

Settled: the bar hugs the edge, band panel inboard — a thin strip on the rim reads as chrome, and it means the band panel's own position relative to the map never changes when the bar grows a row. Costs one renumbering of RESERVER_PRIORITY.

Does the old R feed survive alongside this?

The left-dock command feed and this bar would show the same events in two places, and the feed's 40%-of-dock cap exists to stop it crowding out the selection card.

Settled: retiring the left-dock feed entirely and freeing R to toggle this bar instead. Its KIND_STYLE alert table and signature de-duplication move across; the dock column goes back to the selection card.

Where do four preferences live?

user://narrative.cfg [hud_panels] holds panel toggles; user://band_city_dock.cfg holds the other dockable panel's edge. There are already two files and the panel rules say not to add a third.

Settled: a new [events] section in narrative.cfg holding edge, recent_count, detail_level, channels and suppressed.

Do migrations get events too?

lastEmigrated / lastImmigrated are the only true per-turn people deltas already on the wire — no accumulator needed, they are whole counts today.

Settled: yes, as a Notable migrated kind, since a band losing people to a neighbour is precisely the thing the current diff-based alert misreads as death.

How loud is a death?

The first draft put died on the Alert rung beside a wolf raid. But bands lose elders to cold as a matter of course, and a rung that interrupts for every one of them trains the player to stop reading the bar — the precise failure the three-rung ladder exists to prevent.

Settled: died is Notable. Alert stays for violence, for an investment going feral, and for the client's own faults. A death that matters — a whole band starving out — will announce itself through the starvation and morale channels that already exist.
