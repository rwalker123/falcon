Shadow-Scale · UX proposal · the intensification ladder

Your people learned to break the ground. You didn't notice.

The knowledge tree moved to the Know tab on the Faction panel, which is the right home for it. But nothing tells a player that a track finished, and nothing tells them what the new knowledge lets their hands do. Eight prototypes, one per approach — all live, all in the game's own palette, so the choice can be made by looking rather than by imagining.

Two things already exist, and they change what needs building

Read this before judging the eight

The problem is narrower than “we must tell the player things”. The sentence you want is already written, and the map already answers where. Both land somewhere nobody looks.

1 · The unlock message exists — it goes to the quietest surface in the game

FactionReadouts._announce_knowledge_unlock fires exactly once per track, with a label and a line naming the verb it bought:

as shipped · event dock, System channel
SYS Cultivation learned The Cultivate policy is now available on Thriving wild patches.

One row on a horizontal strip that scrolls, sits behind a three-rung detail floor, and is toggled off entirely with R. The copy is good. The delivery is a surface designed to be ignorable.

2 · The map already marks every source the new knowledge unlocked

SecondaryMarkerRenderer draws ⌃ on any source that can climb a rung, driven off RungGates and the player's own knowledge row. It has never been explained to anyone. That is option E below — the cheapest of the eight, because the feature is done and only the teaching is missing.

3 · Why a 4X popup is a worse fit here than it looks

In Civ the popup is a receipt: you chose Bronze Working fifteen turns ago and have been waiting for it. Here knowledge is earned by practice — you never chose it, you may not know the track exists, and the first you hear of Penning is the turn it lands. A popup has to introduce the noun and the verb simultaneously, to a player who was not waiting for either. That is why several options below spend their effort on anticipation rather than on the moment: a player who has watched Seed Selection ▰▰▰▱▱ 62% climb for ten turns cannot miss it completing.

Which exposes the current Know tab's real defect, and it is not the missing second line: _build_knowledge_block skips any track at progress <= 0.0. A new player opens Know and sees one row, or none. The ladder is invisible until you are already climbing it.

A

The unlock becomes a turn-orb attention row

Fixes the moment · reuses existing machinery · persists until used

The orb is already the generic “something needs you” hub, with a severity-tinted count badge and a popover of rows carrying Jump →. A discovery fits its contract exactly — with one twist that makes it better than every other producer: it stays until you use the verb once, where a note scrolls away.

The discovery row wears the ⌃ the map already uses and the cyan stripe, so it reads as an opportunity rather than as damage — the other two rows are the existing warn/critical producers, for contrast. Not blocking: an unlock must never hold the turn hostage. Press Jump to see it retire.

Buys
A count badge the player already watches every turn; a click that ends with the compose sheet open on a patch you can now cultivate.
Costs
One producer plus a “used it yet” latch beside the existing _knowledge_announced. No new surface, no new art.
Watch
The registry is rebuilt per snapshot; this row is the first that must survive across turns on client state.
B

Know-tab rows go two-line — and the second line is live

Fixes the standing answer · your original idea, with the world's own numbers in it

A static “cultivate a foragable tile” is a manual entry. The client knows the world, so the row can answer where you stand instead: what the knowledge lets you do, and how many places you could do it right now. For a track still climbing, the second line answers the question nothing in the game answers today — what work advances this.

C

Draw the ladder, because that is what it is

Fixes anticipation · the wide-shell rendering of the Know zone

A generic tech-tree DAG would misrepresent this system: there are no prerequisites to shop for, no queue, nothing to click. What a picture is for here is making one arrow visible — the rung you stand on teaches the lesson that unlocks the next one. That rule is the whole economy of the ladder, and it is currently written down only in a Rust doc comment.

hover a rung
Hover any rung to see the lesson working it teaches. Rounded = a lesson. Square = a thing your hands do.

Two webs, not one tree. Foddering hangs below Corral and unlocks no rung — it is what keeping a pen taught you, which is exactly why a generic tree shape would have had to lie about it. The same picture also makes the game's cruellest non-obvious rule readable at a glance: the same hunt teaches Herding on a wild herd and Penning on a tamed one.

Buys
The only option that answers “where is this all going” before the player has climbed anything. Doubles as the teaching surface for how practice works.
Costs
A drawn layout in a zone that is currently rows. Fits the full tier the Discoveries block already branches on.
Watch
Nothing here is clickable in the tech-tree sense — no queue, no choice. If it reads as a planner, it has taught the wrong thing.
D

Spend the moment on The Telling, not on a dialog

Fixes the moment · first-ever unlock only · the most game-specific option here

A modal at turn resolution competes with the four other things that just happened and becomes a click-through. But this game already owns a surface for “something changed about who we are”: the Telling is the book of what happened to us, it already auto-opens for forks, and it is a medium that matures from oral to painted to written.

At the fire · Turn 47

The rains came late and the stands by the river grew thin. Ganna, who had watched the same ground for nine summers, pressed seed back into the broken earth — and the following spring it answered her.

Your people learned Cultivation. They may now cultivate a thriving wild patch; three stand ready. Patches you can work this way wear ⌃ on the map.

Fires on the first track a faction ever learns, and never again — every later unlock takes the quiet path (A). The pigment palette is the Telling's own VOICE_PIGMENT; the beat would be a catalog entry, so it is mod surface rather than engine text.

Buys
The one option that makes learning feel like something that happened to your people rather than a system notification. Introduces the ladder in-fiction, once, at the exact moment it starts mattering.
Costs
Content catalog work per track, plus a first-ever latch. The engine seam for auto-opening already exists (forks use it).
Watch
Prose is the slowest way to deliver a number. Cannot be the only channel — it needs A or B standing behind it for every unlock after the first.
E

Teach the ⌃ mark at the moment it becomes true

Fixes “where” · the feature is already built · cheapest option on this page

The map already marks every source that can climb a rung. On the turn a track completes, those marks change meaning — and that is the one moment a player will read a sentence about them.

the turn Cultivation lands

⌃ — ready to climb

Three patches can be Cultivated now that your people know how. The mark appears on any source whose next rung you can reach.

The marks pulse for the turn the knowledge lands, and the callout is the same sentence the unlock already carries with the glyph named. One-shot teaching, attached to the moment the glyph starts meaning something — strictly better than a legend entry nobody opens. ⚒ is the existing “merely worked” mark, shown for contrast.

Buys
Connects the announcement to the map, where the player must eventually act. Almost free — the renderer exists and is already knowledge-driven.
Costs
A one-turn pulse flag and one extra clause on the unlock copy.
Watch
Not a standalone answer. It makes an existing affordance legible; it does not tell you a track finished.
F

Stop listing knowledge. List what your hands may attempt.

Fixes the standing answer · the reframe · replaces the Know tab's contents

Every other option here treats knowledge as the subject and the verb as its footnote. Invert it. The page becomes a list of verbs — the things your people can do — and knowledge becomes the reason a verb is dark. That answers the actual complaint (“what can I do with it”) by promoting the answer to the primary key.

And it scales — crafting, trade and expeditions all belong on this page, where they would each need their own knowledge sub-list under B.

Buys
The most direct answer to “what can I do with it”, and the only layout that stays right as the verb set grows.
Costs
A new derived model — verbs are currently implied by policies, gates and rungs rather than enumerated anywhere.
Watch
Loses the sense of a ladder. Pairs naturally with C, which supplies exactly that.

The 4X popup, shown honestly

Fixes the moment · the baseline every other option should have to beat

Worth prototyping properly rather than dismissing, because it is the option every player already understands. Here it is at its best — and then at its realistic worst, which is the part that usually goes unprototyped.

Read the bottom line. On the turn this fires, a band also went hungry, a party arrived, a child was born and a pen started starving. The dialog is not alone — it is item two of five, and by the third turn of that the player is clicking through without reading. A Show me ▸ button that jumps to a ready patch rescues a lot of it, and is the half worth stealing regardless of which option wins.

Buys
Genuinely unmissable, once. Zero learning curve — every 4X player knows this dialog.
Costs
A modal layer the HUD does not currently have, plus a queue for the turns that produce several.
Watch
It interrupts, so it trains dismissal; and it introduces a noun and a verb at once to a player who was not waiting for either. If it fires more than a handful of times per game it becomes the thing players mute.
H

An icon off the turn bar, opening the whole tree

Fixes anticipation · your second idea · known and future, one graph

A permanent slot beside the orb, and a single dialog holding everything the faction knows and everything it might. Prototyped with the crafts included, because that is the honest scope — Tanning, Weaving and Bone-working are faction discoveries too, earned the same way.

bottom bar
⌃ Knowledge · 1 new
What your people know 3 known · 2 learning · 4 unknown

Two honest problems this prototype exposes. The graph has almost no edges — three short chains and three orphans — so the graph form is doing very little work that a list would not do better today; it earns its keep only once the tree is much larger. And a permanently present icon is furniture: the player stops seeing it within an hour unless it changes state, and a pip that changes state is option A with a different anchor and no Jump →.

Buys
One place that holds everything, including the crafts the Know tab does not show. Familiar 4X furniture.
Costs
A whole new dialog and layout engine, duplicating what the Know tab is for.
Watch
It is a second home for a fact that already has one, and the hub-vs-rule instinct in this repo says the same about UI: one home per fact.

What I would ship, and in what order

A + E together, as one small change. The unlock stops being a note and becomes an orb row that persists until used and jumps to a real patch; the same copy names the ⌃ the map is already drawing. Between them they answer it happened, what for and where, they add no surface, and the machinery for both already exists.

Then B or F — the same information, differing only in whether knowledge or the verb is the primary key. F is the better page; B is the smaller change. Either one must also stop hiding unstarted tracks, which is a one-condition flip with an outsized effect.

C last, and only if the tab is being rebuilt anyway. D is a genuine flourish worth considering once A is in — it costs catalog content, not engine work.

G and H I would not ship alone. G's dialog is unmissable exactly once and then becomes the thing players click through; H builds a second home for a fact that already has one, and its own prototype shows the graph has barely any edges to draw yet. The half of G worth stealing is the Show me ▸ button, which is what A's Jump → already is.

Three things only you can decide

Does D appeal, or is it too indirect for something this mechanical? It is the most distinctive option here and the one most likely to be wrong.

B or F — is the Know tab about knowledge, with verbs as detail, or about verbs, with knowledge as the reason one is dark?

Is a persistent orb row enough, or do you specifically want something that interrupts? If the latter, I would argue for interrupting once, ever — the first track a player learns — and never again, which is D wearing G's clothes.
