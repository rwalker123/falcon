Issue #731 · arc #527 · UX proposal

Your bone went somewhere and the game never mentioned it.

A band's larder is rebalanced with its neighbours every turn, parties land cargo in it, and a splinter walks off with a dowry — and the only surface that says so is two rows buried in the Food and Fodder popovers. Materials, which is where the interesting movement is, have no account at any layer. This is a fourth dock tab that states what crossed, and three shapes it could take.

01

What exists today, and exactly where it stops

#548 shipped two rows — ⇄ Local exchange and ⇄ Trade route — inside the Food and Fodder disclosures. They name the link the goods crossed, ▲ green for in and ▼ amber for out, and that is the whole of the surface.

The numbers already exist and are thrown away

The material half of balance_supply_networks (supply.rs:604–655) runs one balance_commodity pass per (material, rating) pile and computes each member's signed delta — then applies it to the store and drops it on the floor. The tab does not need a new measurement; it needs the existing one booked.

And the reason the retired Trade: scalar row was never replaced with another row is still true: BandDetailLines.gd:160 — "what a band holds beyond food and fodder is MATERIALS — one pile per material per rating — and that is a LIST, not a vitals row." A tab has the room a row did not.

02

Two levels are honest. A third would be invented.

balance_commodity is a pooled equalization, not pairwise routing: every node above its per-capita fair share ships into one anonymous pot, friction comes off the top, and every node below draws from what is left in proportion to how short it is. There is no fact of the form "Ashfell's bone went to Barrowmere" to render.

⛔ But that is true of the LOCAL arm only — and this changes the design

A route crossing is a party, and a party has a name at both ends. A shipment carries ExpeditionMission::Trade { destination_band, destination_name } (server.rs:6630); the band it lands on knows the party's home_band (expeditions.rs:868). So "12.0 food left for Bitterbrook" is a fact the sim already holds, while "0.8 bone came from Barrowmere" never will be.

The issue states the no-attribution constraint flat across both arms. It is exact for Local and too strong for Route, and Option C below is the layout that spends the difference instead of levelling down to the weaker arm.

So the tab reads at:

  • This band's own net, per commodity, per link kind — exact, per-node, already booked for food and fodder.
  • The network roster, each member with its own net — also exact; every member's delta is computed in the same loop.
  • A route crossing's counterparty, by name — exact, because a party went there.
  • A quantity on a local edge — invented. If one is ever wanted it is a balancer change, not a readout.
Bands have names now

DetailFormat.gd:174 says naming the counterparty was built and rejected because "bands genuinely have no names in this game (issue #615)". #615 is closed — core_sim/src/data/band_names.json ships the curated list and every band carries a real name. The second half of that objection still stands for a row (a variable-length name wraps in a 354px vitals line); it does not stand for a tab. That comment needs correcting whichever option wins.

Most turns are that one. All three layouts have to answer it without reading as broken, and without the player concluding the network is gone. The empty state states the network and the span, then says plainly that nothing moved.

Summed within a material, never across two

The flag's slot (#647)

Every counterparty carries a faction mark — yours and theirs alike, so is this us? has an answer on every row. Try it — 15 camps

The roster does not balance to zero

Click food: two camps shipped 6.7 and this band drew 6.4. The missing 0.3 is friction, taken off the top of the pot by balance_commodity — the cost of the distance, and the one figure on the tab nobody received. A party's cargo has no such list, which is why route rows do not click: a party carried it, from a band that need not be pooling with us.

The span is a floor, not the rule

The header says within 4 tiles, not within 3: since slice 13b the link forms at max(reach_tiles, the route's own reading), so a road extends pooling past the config lever. Copy that hard-codes 3 is already wrong.

My recommendation

C. The asymmetry in §02 is the deciding fact: a route crossing knows its counterparty and a local crossing structurally cannot, and C is the only layout whose shape survives that being true. In A and B the two arms sit in the same list and have to be rendered to the weaker one's standard — every row either omits the peer, or shows it on some rows and not others with no structure explaining why.

C also inherits #548's reasoning rather than re-deciding it: one of these two links the player built, the other happens whether they look or not. That is the sentence the top-level split should make.

04

At fifteen camps

Switch the prototype to 15 camps and 20 parties. Two of the three things on the tab do not grow with the network at all — but they have their own axes, the tab has a fixed height budget, and the line under the stage measures what is rendered against it on every setting.

⛔ First, the premise: fifteen camps cannot be a mixed-faction network

supply::pools_freely gates the union of the component on a.faction == b.faction, and it gates the union rather than the balancing of an already-built component — deliberately, so bands A and C of one people cannot pool through a foreign band B camped between them. So a pooling network of fifteen is fifteen of your own bands, and the roster can never contain a stranger.

A foreign band reaches this tab by exactly one door: resolve_shipment gates a shipment on the connection tie and never asks faction — "a destination in another faction works by construction rather than by a clause someone has to remember to add." So the Route arm is where other peoples appear, and the Local arm structurally cannot show one. In the prototype's large network the shipments run to and from Duskwater, whose faction mark is Obsidian's orange.

The answer to "how does C scale" — measured in the prototype, not argued

C's top-level split survives. Set 15 camps + 20 parties and the tab renders 664px of content: the local arm as one line per good (8), the route arm split into Imports and Exports with each folded to three parties, and the camps behind the network line.

Nothing expands in place. A fold row opens a panel over the zone — the WorkInspectorDialog idiom: an AutoSizingPanel on its own layer, non-modal, no catcher and no scrim, cut back to the map-facing side of the card so it never covers what it came from, and carrying the one ScrollContainer the zone itself may not have. The zone's height therefore does not depend on what the player opened, which is the whole reason the tab can have a fixed budget at all.

At 15 camps and 20 parties the tab is 664px, and that fits a side dock. A 380px side dock's zone has about 1030px to give. A horizontal dock's body is PANEL_HEIGHT_WIDE — 418px, whatever the window height, because the cross axis is the reserved one — so the same content would run 246px past the end. And no cap fixes that: the network line and two arms do not fit 418 however hard their lists are trimmed.

The devices the prototype uses, all of them already idioms in this client

  • One line per good; what it is made of on hover. bone ▲ 0.8 · 6 ratings is the row — the good's net this turn; the six ratings, each with its own amount and direction, are the hover card, beside the row and never under the pointer. The row states what a player scans for; the card states what they check.
  • The route half splits into Imports and Exports before it folds. What came in and what went out are two different questions and a player asks one at a time, so 16 journeys become two ranked lists of three plus two fold rows. Each party is two lines — the counterparty and the turn, then its goods as a clause — rather than a heading and a row per good, which is the parties strip's own trade at seven lines.
  • The camps are a click, not a block. The network line already says 15 camps; that count opens the list, and clicking a good opens the same list scoped to it — each camp's in and out, the ones that moved first, the ones that sat even last, and the friction.
  • A height-starved dock re-authors the zone rather than trimming it. Under ~600px the tab drops to a SHORT tier: the network line, then one row per arm stating what happened — 8 goods · 16 parties (8 imports · 8 exports) — each opening the full list over the map. 152px, and it fits 418 with room to spare. This is the band zone's own idiom: when the height is not there the blocks are re-authored, never generically reflowed.
Why the camps list leads with a count

With 3 camps, who gave me this is nearly determinate. With 15 it is a distribution: the pot is anonymous, and every contributor's share was decided by its own surplus. So the scoped list opens with what stays useful at that size — "10 camps moved it; 4 sat within a tenth of their fair share" — then the camps by how much they moved, then the friction. Every camp's figure is still there; the order is what makes fifteen of them readable.

The one thing 15 camps genuinely breaks

The panel is no-scroll by design — two ScrollContainers are sanctioned (the band zone and the parties zone) and the harness asserts that no other zone has grown one. The Trade zone does not need a third. Its lists are capped, nothing expands in place, and the overflow panel brings its own scroll on its own layer, where a real viewport ceiling makes one legitimate — exactly the exemption BandComposeFloat already holds.

What the horizontal dock needs instead is the SHORT tier: 664px does not fit 418 and no cap makes it, so the zone becomes three rows and a way in. That is a second authored tier to build and keep working, which is the real cost of this answer.

The 418 and 1030 are the shipped PANEL_HEIGHT_WIDE and a side dock at a 1200px window — close enough to decide the shape, but the figure to build against is the live zone_size(), measured, not an HTML mock's scrollHeight.

05

The producer split has six causes, not three

The issue scopes a producer split — local pooling, an arriving shipment, an expedition's launch draw — because one counter covers several different events. Grepping every writer of the ledger turns up six, and they do not collapse to three:

A band's own party coming home is not trade — and the Band panel says it is

The drop-off and the fold-back book on the Route arm because a party carried the goods, which keeps the ledger consistent. But to a player they are the band's own hunting and foraging, not a trade route, so the Trade tab never shows them: it states crossings with somebody else.

The Band panel has the same problem today. Its Food popover renders the whole Route arm as ⇄ Trade route (DisclosureController.gd:245), so a hunt's haul coming home is labelled as trade — and it is not in the hunting income either, since the sim books it as "neither income nor consumption". The cause split is what fixes both: with a cause key on each crossing, the homecoming moves out of ⇄ Trade route and into the Food popover beside the hunts and forages it came from.

Nor is a split's dowry

When a band splits, the parent hands the splinter a share of its stores. It books on the Local arm because the splinter starts in its parent's camp — but it is a one-off the player chose by splitting, not an exchange, so the Trade tab leaves it out too. Its place is the split's own announcement and the new band's panel.

A defect the split will expose

server.rs:6618 debits the launch as cargo + provisions — the shipment's goods and the party's own walking rations, in one number. Under a cause split those are two different events: the cargo is a transfer to another band, the rations are a larder the party carries and folds back home if unspent. A row reading "Shipment to Bitterbrook — 12.0" would be overstating the shipment by whatever the party is eating on the way.

I will split that term when I implement it, unless you want it left alone. It is a one-line change at the debit and a second cause key on the wire; nothing downstream reads the combined figure except the row that is being replaced.

06

A fourth zone is not free on a bottom dock

BandCityPanel picks its shell off the panel's own width against wide_shell_min_width(), which is derived from the live zone list: each zone's flank, plus one separator span per gap, plus the card chrome. A band declares three today:

Today — 3 zones380 + 380 + 354 + 2×25 + 26 = 1190
With a Trade zone380 + 380 + 354 + 354 + 3×25 + 26 = 1569

So a bottom dock between 1190 and 1569px would stop showing its zones abreast and start tabbing — a regression at 1366, 1440 and 1536, which is most laptops. The Trade zone is the one zone whose content is empty on most turns, so it would be buying that with the least.

Three ways out, and what each costs

(i) Declare it anyway — simplest, honest, and the wide shell degrades for a band of window widths. (ii) Declare it only when something crossed — the threshold moves per turn, which means the shell can flip mid-game on a turn tick; that is the map-flicker failure the fixed cross-axis size exists to prevent, so I would not. (iii) Declare it on the narrow shell only, and on a wide shell append the Trade content as a section under Parties — one extra section in an existing scrolling zone, no new flank, threshold unchanged at 1190.

I would take (iii), and it is a small change to BandPanelController's two layout branches, which already differ. But it is a genuine trade — under (iii) the Trade content is one scroll down on a bottom dock rather than its own column — so it is yours to call. The prototype's Dock toggle draws both, each at the real strip width its zone list demands, so the difference is something to look at rather than to take on arithmetic.

08

Decisions I need from you

Which layout

A, B or C. I recommend C for the reason in §03 — it is the only one that survives a route crossing knowing its counterparty and a local crossing not.

Route crossings name the band at the other end — yes or no

This is the substantive departure from the issue, which bars per-peer attribution flat. It is exact for the Local arm and too strong for Route (§02). If you want the blanket rule kept anyway, say so and C loses most of its advantage — then I would build A.

The wide shell

(i) a real fourth flank and the 1190 → 1569 threshold move, or (iii) tab-only with the content appended under Parties on a wide dock. I recommend (iii).

The SHORT tier on a horizontal dock

664px does not fit 418px and no cap makes it, so a height-starved dock either gets the SHORT tier (three rows stating what happened per arm, each opening the full list) or the Trade tab is simply not offered on a bottom dock. I recommend the tier — it is the band zone's existing idiom — but it is a second authored layout to build and keep working, so it is worth saying no to if you would rather the tab were vertical-dock only.

The cargo + provisions debit

Split it (my default), or leave it and have the shipment row overstate by the party's rations.
